# Clear Communication

## Communication as a system

Communication is not a moment. It’s a system.

Messages get repeated. Meaning decays. Gaps get filled with assumptions.

If you’re not tired of repeating something, your team hasn’t internalized it yet.

Clarity is not what you say once. It’s what people hear—and what they keep hearing—not only what you said in the room.

## Intent, action, and impact: close the loop until it’s mutual

Most communication breaks here.

Your intent is private. Your actions are visible—words, including non-verbal cues, such as timing, tone, what you don’t follow up on. Your impact is what others experience.

Leaders speak from intent. Teams react to impact.

Your job is not just to say things clearly. It’s to check what actually landed and close the gap until understanding is mutual. That takes reflective listening and explicit loops, not another monologue.

## Say what you know and what you don’t; name trade-offs; label the conversation

You don’t need perfect answers. You need to orient people.

Here’s what we know.  
Here’s what we don’t.  
Here’s what we’re doing next.

Avoid false confidence.

Name the trade-offs:

What you’re prioritizing.  
What you’re giving up.

Label the conversation so people know what kind of moment this is:

This is a discussion.  
This is a decision.  
This is direction.

## Mindful talking and reflective listening when you’re actively aligning

People don’t hear what you say. They hear it through their context, their biases, and what they’re worried about.

Communication is not complete when you speak. It’s complete when it’s understood.

Close the loop:

What I’m hearing is…  
It sounds like…

Mindful talking in those alignment moments means:

Pay attention to tone.  
Be intentional about timing.  
Acknowledge emotional context.

Don’t ignore emotion. Silence communicates too.

## The right channel for the job

Different channels solve different problems.

1:1s  
Resolve ambiguity and surface what won’t be said openly.

When 1:1s go missing  
Hear what your system is not telling you.

Team forums and meetings  
Align direction and make trade-offs visible.

Written updates  
Scale clarity across time zones and memory.

Short videos  
Add tone and intent, especially for distributed teams.

Choose the channel for the job, not only for your comfort.

## When repetition serves clarity

This is where communication starts to work as a system.

Same substance, calibrated by audience and setting—not a new message every week for the sake of novelty.

Say it across the places people already gather. Adapt examples and emphasis, not the core trade-off. Reinforce it over time.

You know it’s working when people can predict you—when they cross paths with you and already know your first question. When they start saying the thing back in their own words.

That’s alignment. Repetition here serves clarity, not noise.

---

Closing

Good communication is not about saying things once. It’s about creating shared understanding over time.

Clarity isn’t when you’ve said it. It’s when others start saying it for you.
